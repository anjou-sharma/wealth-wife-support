wealth wife

Support

Wealth Wife turns something you already do into money for Future You. You set what an activity is worth, you do it, and you decide whether to move the money. The app never moves anything itself.

Getting started

  1. Write a rule — the activity, and what it's worth to you. Fifty cents a minute at the gym. Fifteen dollars for a therapy session. Whatever number feels right.
  2. Tap the dial on Home to start it, or log it afterwards if it already happened.
  3. When you finish, the app works out what it came to.
  4. Send it to a goal, or skip. Skipping is always fine.

Examples to start from

Rates are only suggestions. Yours should be whatever the thing is actually worth to you, and you can change any of them later.

By the minute

A timer runs while you do it, and the value climbs. Good for anything with a length: the gym at $0.50 a minute, a walk at $0.20, studying for an exam at $0.40, guitar practice at $0.30, meditating at $0.60, cleaning the apartment at $0.25.

By the hour

The same idea, priced the way you'd think about it: volunteering at $20 an hour, a shift you picked up at your actual rate, a long drive at $15, time in the garden at $12.

A flat amount

For things that either happen or don't, where length isn't the point. A therapy appointment at $25. Cooking dinner rather than ordering, at $18. Getting to bed before midnight, at $10. An evening with your phone in a drawer, at $8. Calling your grandmother, at $5.

Per unit

When you count them rather than time them: $2 a mile run, $0.50 a lap swum, $3 a chapter read, $1 a set at the gym, $5 a job application sent.

A price you enter

For amounts that change every time. The coffee you bought, at whatever it cost that day. A subscription you cancelled, at its monthly price. A repair you did yourself, at what the shop quoted. A dress you put back on the rack, at the price on the tag.

Goals worth naming

Future Me, an emergency fund, a trip you keep talking about, a down payment, grad school, a wedding, a car, someone else entirely. A goal can hold a target and a date if you want one, or just a running total if you don't.

Common questions

Does the app connect to my bank?

No. It never connects to a bank, a broker, or any account, and it cannot move money. When you mark something as sent, you move the money yourself in whatever app you already use.

Where is my data kept?

On your iPhone, and nowhere else. There is no account and no server.

Can I get my data back after deleting it?

No. Because nothing is stored off your device, deleting the app or using Delete All Data removes it permanently. There is no backup.

How do I change the language?

Settings, then Appearance, then Language. Wealth Wife is available in ten languages and follows your iPhone's language by default.

What happens if I skip?

Nothing. The session is recorded as skipped and no money is counted as moved. There are no streaks and nothing is held against you.

What is the monthly limit?

An optional ceiling on how much you mark as sent in a month. Amounts past it are still recorded in full, they just aren't counted as moved. They are not a debt and they don't roll over. You can turn the limit off entirely.

Does Compare predict what will happen?

No. Compare runs simulations from numbers you enter. It is not a forecast, it is not based on any real investment's history, and Wealth Wife never recommends a stock, fund, or any other investment.

Why do my reminders arrive at odd times?

Reminders are scheduled on your device by iOS. Check Settings, then Reminders, in the app, and confirm notifications are allowed for Wealth Wife in your iPhone's Settings.
